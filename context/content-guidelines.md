# Content Guidelines

## Voice

Confident, direct, slightly technical. Sounds like a smart friend who builds things, not a marketing agency.

**Yes:** "We build a WhatsApp bot that talks to your customers when you're asleep."
**No:** "We deliver AI-powered conversational solutions that maximize customer engagement around the clock."

## Plain language is the law

The target audience is Malaysian SME owners. Many are not fluent in English. Every piece of copy on the site must:

- Aim for Grade 6–8 reading level (Flesch reading ease 60+)
- Use short sentences (under 20 words where possible)
- Prefer everyday words over "sophisticated" ones
- Avoid idioms that don't translate
- Avoid long subordinate clauses

**Word swaps:**

| Instead of | Use |
|---|---|
| utilize | use |
| leverage | use |
| implement | build, set up |
| solution | tool, app, system |
| optimize | make faster, make better |
| stakeholders | customers, team, owners |
| synergy | — (delete it entirely) |
| cutting-edge | new, modern |
| robust | strong, reliable |
| scalable | grows with you |
| bespoke | custom, made for you |
| seamless | smooth, easy |
| empower | help |
| drive | get, bring |

## Banned words and phrases

Never use, regardless of context:

- "World-class," "best-in-class," "industry-leading"
- "Revolutionary," "game-changing," "disruptive"
- "Cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," "next-generation"
- "Synergy," "holistic," "ecosystem" (when not meaning a literal ecosystem)
- "Solutions" as a noun for what we sell
- "In today's fast-paced world" or any variant
- "Let us help you unlock…" — just no

## Sentence length

**Target average: 14 words.** Mix short and medium sentences. Avoid long ones unless the idea demands it.

Paragraphs: 2–4 sentences. One idea per paragraph.

## Numbers

**Don't publish specific numbers in case studies.** No customer counts, no revenue figures, no response-time improvements, no lead volumes — this is a hard rule, not a default. Do not break it even if the client shares an impressive metric.

Default to qualitative descriptions:

**Yes:** "Response times improved to near-instant."
**No:** "Response times dropped 87% from 4 hours to 31 seconds."

**Yes:** "The tool replaced hours of manual data entry every week."
**No:** "Saves 12 hours per week."

**Yes:** "Live with paying customers."
**No:** "6 paying customers across 14 branches."

### The metrics strip

The case study layout in `DESIGN.md` §8 includes a "metrics strip" component. Keep the layout, but fill it with qualitative signals, not numbers. Good fills:

- "Live customer" / "Paying users"
- "Daily runs" / "Runs every morning"
- "Production-grade"
- "In market since 2026"
- "Built in 6 weeks" (scope signals are fine — these describe the build, not the result)
- "Used across multiple branches"

Bad fills (never use):

- "6 branches"
- "RM50K+ processed"
- "40% faster"
- "200+ leads/month"

If a number is ever approved for publication on a specific case study, it requires explicit written sign-off from Chris and lives in the case study's data file with a comment documenting the approval.

## Case study structure

Each case study page follows this order:

1. **Title** — the project name
2. **Micro-label** — category tag (e.g. `A U T O M A T I O N`)
3. **One-line summary** — what it is, in under 15 words
4. **The problem** — 2–3 short paragraphs, in the client's voice where possible
5. **What we built** — 2–3 paragraphs, plain language
6. **The outcome** — qualitative, no specific numbers (unless approved)
7. **Tech stack** — simple list, icons or logos if visual
8. **Visuals** — 2–4 screenshots or diagrams
9. **Related work** — 2 other case studies to click through to
10. **CTA** — "Want something similar built? Talk to us."

## CTAs

Primary CTA everywhere: **"Talk to us"** (links to WhatsApp or contact form).

Secondary CTAs:

- "See how it works" (to a services page)
- "Read the full story" (to a case study)
- "Read more" (blog post index to individual post)

Never use:

- "Get started now"
- "Unlock your potential"
- "Book your free consultation today"
- "Don't miss out"

## Blog post voice

Same rules as above, plus:

- **Lead with the answer, not the setup.** If the post answers "How do I automate Unifi leads?", the first paragraph should start answering. Don't spend 200 words on "In today's world…"
- **Use concrete examples.** "A reseller in Klang Valley…" beats "Businesses often…"
- **Subheadings are navigation.** Someone skimming should get the point from h2s alone.
- **End with a clear next step.** What should the reader do after reading? Link to it.

## Malaysian context

The audience is Malaysian. That means:

- Malaysian English conventions where they differ from US English (e.g. "favour" vs "favor" — stay consistent, pick UK spelling)
- Malaysian Ringgit (RM) for any prices, not USD
- Local references land better than generic ones — "a kopitiam owner in PJ" beats "a small business owner"
- Don't assume reader knows US-specific cultural references

## Proofreading

Before any copy ships:

1. Read it aloud. If you stumble, rewrite that sentence.
2. Check reading level (Hemingway Editor or similar — aim for Grade 6–8).
3. Check for banned words (see list above).
4. Check for unpublished numbers (see rules above).
5. Verify all claims are accurate and approved.
